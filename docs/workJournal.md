## 2026-10-27 — The privacy links' colour pins become contrast against the ground they sit on (#86)

> Superseded in part by 2026-11-01 — The privacy contrast check becomes a computed-style read (#86).

The fleet test audit (`reddoorla/reddoor-workspace` `audits/2026-10-26-fleet-test-audit/report.md`, batch D) flagged the two "links to /privacy" tests in `tests/privacy/page.spec.ts`: each pinned the Privacy Policy link's colour equal to its parent's, a design value a human may change. The audit's rewrite asserted WCAG contrast ≥ 4.5 instead, but its helper walked up to the first opaque `background-color`. For the footer that is `body`'s black, because the footer sits on `finalBg.jpg`, an `<img>` no computed style can see, so it scored white on black (21:1) whatever the photo did. It measured the wrong ground.

The helper measures the ground and resolves the text colour. It waits for `load` so the photo is there, screenshots the element's box as rendered, with its text hidden (fill, `-webkit-text-stroke`, `text-shadow` and decoration transparent), and as rendered again, and decodes them on a blank page (out of the site's CSP). The text colour is the computed `color`, or the stroke colour when the fill is transparent, parsed by a canvas so `rgba` and `oklch` both work, with its alpha multiplied by the `opacity` of the element and every ancestor. It is composited over each pixel of the hidden shot and scored against it, and the verdict is the worst tenth of those per-pixel ratios. It throws, naming the element and its colours, when the text changes no pixel, no colour resolves, the box resizes or the two rendered shots differ (a ground that moves). Two earlier versions in this PR were wrong, and review caught each. The first parsed the computed `color` as three numbers, so a 25%-alpha white link (2.00:1 as rendered) passed and `text-neutral-200`, which Chrome reports in `oklch`, became NaN. The second read the text colour from the pixels the glyphs changed, which made the verdict depend on antialiasing: at the footer's 12 px, `text-[#808080] no-underline` (5.26 nominal) scored 4.32, and weight 200 scored 2.42 at DPR 1 but 5.33 at DPR 2. It also scored every text against the ground's lighter tenth, so dark text over a half-dark ground passed at 19.17, and it left a stroke in the ground shot, so white outline text scored 1.00. Glyph pixels are the wrong instrument for a colour the browser already knows; they remain only as the check that the text renders at all. There is no overlay on that photo to use as a token; measuring the pixels was the only way to see the photo at all. Behind the footer link the photo is near-black: mean RGB (7, 7, 7), median contrast 21:1 and the 95th-percentile pixel still 16.5:1 against white, at 1280×720, 390×664 and 1920×1080. In the dialog the ground is the panel's `bg-black`, as before.

Proved against a production bundle (`vite build`, 30 s here): the footer link given `text-dark` goes red at 2.36; the photo given `invert`, so the footer's white text sits on a light ground, goes red at 1.00; the notice given `text-dark` goes red at 2.39. Run against the same inverted photo, the audit's helper passed, which is the defect, shown. After review 1, on the dev server, the alpha link and an `opacity-25` link go red at 2.00, `text-neutral-200` passes, and `text-transparent` throws naming the footer's privacy link. After review 2, on the dev server at DPR 1 and 2 and at 1280×720 and 390×664, the verdicts do not move with either: `text-[#808080] no-underline` passes at 5.26 to 5.32, weight 200 `#8a8a8a` at 6.01 to 6.08, white stroke-only text at 20.77 to 21; the half-dark gradient goes red at 1.00, alpha and `opacity-25` at 2.02 to 2.03. A 30 ms interval flickering the photo's brightness throws "its ground moved". The whole spec passed 5 times over on a production build (30 of 30). One mutant was equivalent and taught something: `text-dark` on the `<footer>` itself changes nothing, because the unlayered `.label` rule in `app.css` sets `color: #fff` and beats every Tailwind utility on that element.

After review 3 the helper became an allowlist. Round 3 showed that the model, the text drawn as `color × opacity` and composited normally, broke at every other compositing step, and each break gave a confident number in one direction or the other. `filter: brightness(.3)`, `filter: opacity(25%)` and `mix-blend-mode: overlay` rendered the link at 1.6 to 2.7:1 and it scored 20.77. A child `<span style="color:#333">` and `-webkit-text-fill-color: #333` were left in the "ground" shot, so the underline was scored instead (15.91). The site's own `.outlined` halo scored 1.00. Ancestor opacity was counted twice: a footer with `background:#fff; opacity:.8` and a `#555` link renders at 6.06 and scored 3.23. Inline colour styles were removed rather than restored, so `style="color:#ccc"` threw "its ground moved". Each of these patched in place would have been the next round's finding, so the helper now refuses what it does not model and names it. On the text or any ancestor, `filter`, `mix-blend-mode`, `mask-image` and `background-clip: text` throw, as do, on the text itself, `text-shadow` (a halo is the text's ground by WCAG and a drop shadow is not, and no pixel tells them apart), a stroke of another colour round a visible fill, and `::before`/`::after` text. `backdrop-filter` is modelled, because it acts on the ground the screenshot already carries. Every element in the link that paints a text node of its own is scored on its own, and the worst wins. The text is hidden with `-webkit-text-fill-color`, not `color`, and a shown element gets its own paint set back explicitly, because a hidden parent's transparent fill is inherited (the first draft of this fix measured the `#333` span as "changes no pixel" for exactly that reason). Opacity is now exact: with H the ground as rendered and U the ground with the faded ancestors lifted to opacity 1, the text renders at H + O·alpha·(text − U), which gives 5.93 at 1280 and 6.06 at 390 for the footer case above. Renovate's lock file maintenance (#92, into `staging`) went red on 2026-11-02 and stayed red after its 2026-11-03 rebase: `svelte-check found 41 errors`, every one of them "Cannot find name 'process'" or "Cannot find name 'node:fs'", in `sveltekit.config.js`, `playwright.config.ts` and the smoke specs, plus implicit-`any` errors that follow from them. This repo never declared `@types/node`. The old lockfile resolved it only as some dependency's optional peer, and the refresh dropped it, which is how a lockfile-only change took away the types the config files rely on. Reddoor's site broke the same way on the same night (reddoorla/reddoor-website#314). Declaring `@types/node` (`^26.6.6`, the major the old lockfile had) as a devDependency fixes it. Measured on #92's own branch with the line added: `svelte-check` went from 41 errors to 0 (the 47 warnings were there before and do not fail CI), and `pnpm lint` and `pnpm build` passed. The same result held on `staging` with the line added. Once this lands on `staging`, #92 rebases onto it.

## 2026-11-03 — `@types/node` follows the runtime's Node 24, not 26

The entry above declared `@types/node` at `^26.6.6`, calling it "the major the old lockfile had". Review then pointed out that 26 was only what pnpm had picked for an optional peer, which nobody chose. The site runs on Node 24 (`.nvmrc` 24.22.2, CI `node-version: "24"`, Netlify `NODE_VERSION = "24"`). Types for Node 26 would let code that calls a Node 25 or 26 API pass `svelte-check` and then fail on Netlify. The fix was written after #97 had already merged at `^26`, so it lands here as its own change: `@types/node` is now `^24` (24.19.2), and `undici-types` moves to 7.24.6 with it. Measured on `staging` at `bdc1d0b`: `svelte-check` 0 errors (the 47 warnings were there before), and `pnpm lint` and `pnpm build` pass.

## 2026-11-04 — svelte-select leaves with the /portfolios demo; svelte-gestures stays (#99)

`svelte-select` had one real importer, `/portfolios`. That page is a wireframer-scaffold demo: six "Item 1…6" cards over lorem ipsum, behind a Dev/UX/UI multi-select. Nothing linked to it, but it served 200 with no `noindex`. Two `FullWidth/Styled*Select.svelte` wrappers also imported it, and nothing imported them. On 11-04 the operator chose removal over a bump to 6, the only release in 31 months and still not a WAI-ARIA combobox (workspace `decisions/2026-11-04-1740-select-gestures-keep-or-replace.md`). So the route, its smoke entry, both wrappers, the svelte-select CSS and the dependency go. With them go two files only the route or wrappers used: `icons/dropdown-arrow-light.svg`, and `ScreenWidth/ScreenWidthGallerySliderLarge.svelte`, which an Opus review of the diff found orphaned. The lockfile lost 38 lines: `svelte-select`, `svelte-floating-ui` and `@floating-ui/*`, and nothing else.

One belief corrected on contact. The brief said to delete the CSS block that opened at `app.css:286` whole. That block also held a global `::placeholder` rule, and that rule styles the live home-page contact form: NAME, EMAIL and YOUR MESSAGE are set in Chakra Petch, 14px, uppercase, `#c2d1d9`. The rule stays, with a comment saying what it styles. The rest of the block was select-only, plus `input[type="textarea"]::placeholder`, which matches nothing.

`svelte-gestures` 5.2.2 stays: the home page's mobile "Evolving Digital Age" carousel swipes through `src/lib/utils/swipeAction.ts`. That carousel's dots are `<div onclick aria-hidden>`, out of reach of the keyboard and screen readers. That is filed as #100 and not fixed here.
